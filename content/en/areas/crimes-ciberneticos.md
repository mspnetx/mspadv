## Criminal law in the digital age

Technology has brought new kinds of crime — and new kinds of accusation. Cybercrime investigations involve complex technical evidence, such as IP records, device forensics and provider data, which calls for a specialized defense.

Both victims and those accused need precise technical guidance to deal with digital evidence, which may be fragile, badly collected or wrongly interpreted.

### Cases we handle

- Hacking of computer devices (Article 154-A of the Criminal Code).
- Electronic fraud and scams (PIX payments, e-commerce, social media).
- Crimes against honor committed online.
- Leaks and misuse of personal data.
- Accusations involving digital content and electronic evidence.

> Digital evidence is not always what it seems. A technical review of the chain of custody can be decisive for the defense.

## How we work

We examine digital evidence critically, question its validity and how it was obtained, work with forensic experts when needed, and build the defense on the technical weaknesses of the material presented.

::: faq
? Is an IP address enough for a conviction?
Not necessarily. An IP address points to an access point, not to the person responsible. The defense can show the weaknesses of that link.

? I was the victim of an online scam. Can you help?
Yes. We advise on the criminal measures available, on preserving evidence and on the routes to holding those responsible to account.

? What is the chain of custody of digital evidence?
It is the record of how the evidence was collected, stored and handled. Failures in that process can undermine the validity of the evidence in the case.

? Can a digital offense lead to prison?
Yes, several cybercrimes carry prison sentences. That is why a technical defense from the start of the investigation is essential.
:::
