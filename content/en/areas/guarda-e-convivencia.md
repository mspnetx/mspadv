## When a separation involves children, the strategy changes

In disputes over children there are no winners in the usual sense: there are children who need stability, routine and the presence of both parents whenever that is safe. Brazilian law adopts shared custody as the rule, but how the rule applies depends on each family's circumstances.

The lawyer's job is to turn the family's reality into a workable arrangement — and to act quickly when the child is at risk or when what was decided is not being respected.

### Situations we handle

- Establishing shared or sole custody.
- Setting and reviewing parenting time (visits, holidays, special dates).
- Relocation with the child to another city or country.
- Parental alienation.
- Breach of an agreement or court order on parenting time.
- Cases involving a risk of domestic or family violence.

> Shared custody does not mean splitting time exactly in half: it means that the important decisions about the child's life are made by both parents.

## How we work

We assess the child's routine and each parent's situation, propose a realistic parenting plan and seek agreement. When the matter is urgent — a risk to the child's safety, wrongful retention or a sudden change of residence — we request immediate relief such as interim custody, a recovery order or interim parenting time.

::: faq
? Is shared custody mandatory in Brazil?
It is the rule when both parents are fit to exercise it, even without agreement between them. It is not applied when one parent states that they do not want custody, or when there are indications of a risk of domestic or family violence.

? Does shared custody mean no child support?
No. Custody and support are separate matters. Support is still set according to the child's needs and each parent's means.

? Can I move to another city or country with my child?
A move that affects parenting time must be agreed with the other parent or authorized by the court. Leaving the country with the child without authorization can have serious consequences, including international ones.

? What can I do if the other parent prevents visits?
Keep a record of each episode and seek advice. You can ask the court to enforce the order, with fines and other measures, and to examine possible parental alienation.
:::
